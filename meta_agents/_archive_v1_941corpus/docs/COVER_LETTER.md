To the Editors, *PLOS ONE*

Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, **"Racial and Ethnic Disparities in Breast Cancer Incidence in the United States: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Showing That Aggregate Racial Categories Conceal Ethnic, Molecular-Subtype, and Age-Specific Disparities,"** for publication in *PLOS ONE* as a Research Article.

US cancer surveillance routinely reports breast cancer incidence in a handful of aggregate race/ethnicity categories, and the recently reported "convergence" of Black and White incidence has been read as a narrowing of disparity. In this systematic review and meta-analysis of 27 population-based studies (14 in quantitative synthesis), I show that this reassuring aggregate picture is an artefact of how the data are grouped. When incidence is disaggregated, the single "Asian/Pacific Islander" category spans a roughly three-fold range (Korean 0.34 to Native Hawaiian 1.11 versus non-Hispanic White); the near-null Black–White aggregate resolves into a steep molecular-subtype gradient (up to a two-fold excess of triple-negative disease in Black women) that is ethnicity-specific rather than a uniform minority effect; and the reported "convergence" is a lifelong age crossover that age-standardization averages to near unity. Every disparity direction was robust across fixed-effect, leave-one-out, and one-estimate-per-registry-family sensitivity analyses that directly address the non-independence of overlapping US registries.

I believe the work is a good fit for *PLOS ONE* for two reasons. First, its contribution is methodological and generalizable — it uses meta-analytic heterogeneity itself to demonstrate *why* aggregate categories mislead, with direct implications for how surveillance agencies and etiologic researchers should report and stratify race/ethnicity data. Second, it embodies *PLOS ONE*'s emphasis on transparency and reproducibility: the complete dataset, analysis code (meta-analysis, sensitivity analyses, risk-of-bias and GRADE assessments, and figure generation), search protocol, and a value-by-value extraction log are openly available, and the entire analysis reproduces from a clean environment.

This manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and the author has approved the submission. I have no competing interests to declare. The review was not prospectively registered, as noted in the Methods.
