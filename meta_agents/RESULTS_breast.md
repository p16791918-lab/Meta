# Results — Racial/Ethnic Disparities in Breast Cancer Incidence

Random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird) meta-analysis of age-adjusted / age-standardized
**invasive breast-cancer incidence** rates by race/ethnicity, reference = White / NHW.
Method A (within-study IRR) pooled across studies by group and outcome.
Generated by `run_meta_analysis_breast.py`; data in `extracted_studies_breast.md`.

## Headline finding

The racial disparity in breast cancer is **subtype-specific, not a
whole-disease excess**:

- **Overall** invasive incidence is *lower* in every minority group than in
  White women (Black 0.69, Hispanic 0.70, Asian/PI 0.74, AIAN 0.68).
- **Triple-negative (TNBC)** incidence is the exception: **Black women have
  ~1.9× the White rate**, while Hispanic, Asian/PI and AIAN women remain below
  White women even for TNBC.

So the well-known Black–White breast-cancer disparity is concentrated in the
aggressive TNBC / ER-negative subtype, consistent with the poorer Black
survival reported elsewhere — not in overall incidence.

## Heterogeneity notes

- **Overall Black vs White (I²=99.9%)**: driven by geography — US Black women have
  near-parity (IRR 0.94, SEER), whereas Black African women in England (0.59) and
  Black women in Brazil (0.59) are far lower. The pooled 0.69 straddles these; the
  prediction interval (0.33–1.44) shows the true effect depends heavily on setting.
  This is a genuine population difference, not noise.
- **TNBC Black vs White** is remarkably consistent (IRR 1.81–1.95 across SEER 2010,
  SEER 2010–2019, and national USCS 2015–2019).

## Age crossover (context, not pooled)

Overall incidence hides an age crossover (Anderson 2008; Xu 2024): among women
**<40–50 y** Black incidence *exceeds* White (IRR up to 1.53 at 20–29 y), then
crosses over so that White exceeds Black at older ages — which is why the
age-adjusted overall IRR sits near/below 1 while young-onset disease is worse
in Black women.

## Included studies (data source)

Overall incidence: Wang 2022 (SEER 2000–2018), England 2025 (NCRAS 2011–2019),
Brazil 2024 (13 registries 2010–2015).
TNBC: Xie 2023 (SEER 2010–2019), Kong 2023 (USCS/NPCR-SEER 2015–2019),
Yao 2013 (SEER 2010). ER-negative: Jenkins 2025 (SEER 2013–2015).

## Key limitations

1. **Abstract-level extraction.** NCBI/PubMed is blocked by the environment's
   network policy, so rates were taken from Embase abstracts; full-text
   verification of a few numbers (and group-specific case counts for Poisson SEs,
   which were estimated where not reported) is pending.
2. **Non-independence.** US SEER/USCS studies share a registry and overlapping
   years, so pooled CIs understate true uncertainty. The tiny within-study
   variances (huge registries) inflate I² and give the pooled estimate
   artificially tight CIs — interpret pooled points as direction/magnitude
   summaries, not as independent-sample confidence statements.
3. **Screening-detection confounding.** Overall incidence partly reflects
   mammography access, which differs by race/ethnicity — a driver of the
   incidence gradient that this design cannot separate.
4. Prevalence and mortality were excluded by protocol (incidence-only review).
