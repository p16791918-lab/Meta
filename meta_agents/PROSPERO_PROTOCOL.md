### ✅PASTE 13. Review question
In the United States, how does invasive female breast cancer incidence differ by
race/ethnicity relative to non-Hispanic White women, and do aggregate race/
ethnicity categories differ from estimates disaggregated by detailed ethnicity
(e.g., Asian subgroups), molecular subtype, and age?

### ✅PASTE 14. Searches
We will search PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science Core Collection
from 1 January 2000 to 31 December 2025, limited to English-language human
studies. Three concept blocks — breast cancer; race/ethnicity/disparity;
incidence/age-adjusted rate — combined with a US-context block, using
title/abstract/keyword fields and controlled vocabulary. Reviews, letters,
editorials, notes, and conference abstracts will be excluded. Reference lists of
included studies will be screened. Full database-specific strings are provided in
a supplementary appendix.

### ✅PASTE 15. Condition or domain being studied
Invasive female breast cancer incidence (age-adjusted) in the United States.

### ✅PASTE 16. Participants/population
Population-based (cancer-registry) female populations in the United States.
Inclusion: studies reporting age-adjusted invasive breast cancer incidence for
≥1 US racial/ethnic minority group relative to non-Hispanic White women.
Exclusion: mortality, survival, prevalence, in-situ-only, stage-specific-only,
male breast cancer, mortality-to-incidence ratios, and non-US studies.

### ✅PASTE 17. Intervention(s) or exposure(s)
Race/ethnicity (self-identified or registry-classified), including disaggregated
ethnic subgroups, molecular subtype, nativity, and age band.

### ✅PASTE 18. Comparator(s)/control
Non-Hispanic White women (reference group).

### ✅PASTE 19. Types of study to be included
Observational population-based/registry studies (cross-sectional incidence and
registry trend analyses) reporting age-adjusted incidence rates or rate ratios.
Excluded: reviews, editorials, letters, notes, conference abstracts, case reports.

### ▢PICK 20. Context — paste: US cancer surveillance systems (SEER, NAACCR, USCS, state/regional registries).

### ✅PASTE 21. Main outcome(s)
Within-study incidence rate ratio (IRR) = minority age-adjusted incidence ÷
non-Hispanic White age-adjusted incidence (age-adjusted rate per 100,000; IRR
with 95% CI).

### ✅PASTE 22. Additional outcome(s)
Subtype-specific IRRs (HR+/HER2−, HR+/HER2+, HER2-enriched, triple-negative,
ER-status); age-band-specific rate ratios; nativity-stratified IRRs; disaggregated
Asian/Pacific Islander ethnic-subgroup IRRs.

### ✅PASTE 23. Data extraction (selection and coding)
One reviewer will screen titles/abstracts then full texts and extract data, with
AI-assisted support for retrieval, de-duplication, and first-pass classification;
all screening decisions and every extracted value will be verified by the reviewer
against the source article. Items: age-adjusted rates, IRR/RR, 95% CI or case
counts/person-years, registry, region, calendar period, race/ethnicity categories,
subtype, age band.

### ✅PASTE 24. Risk of bias (quality) assessment
Risk of bias of included observational studies will be assessed with the
Newcastle–Ottawa Scale.

### ✅PASTE 25. Strategy for data synthesis
Random-effects meta-analysis of within-study log-IRRs (restricted maximum
likelihood; Hartung–Knapp adjustment in sensitivity analysis), with I², τ², and
95% prediction intervals. Because US registries overlap, one estimate per
registry family will be used in the primary analysis, with all-studies and
overlap-excluded analyses as sensitivity checks. Certainty via GRADE.

### ✅PASTE 26. Analysis of subgroups or subsets
By detailed ethnicity (Asian/Pacific Islander subgroups), molecular subtype, age
band, and nativity.
