**11. Review question**
In the United States, how does invasive female breast cancer incidence differ by
race/ethnicity relative to non-Hispanic White women, and do aggregate race/
ethnicity categories conceal disparities that are visible only after
disaggregation by (a) detailed ethnicity (e.g., Asian subgroups), (b) molecular
subtype, and (c) age?

**12. Searches**
PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science Core Collection, from 1 January
2000 to 31 December 2025, English-language, human studies. Three concept blocks
(breast cancer; race/ethnicity/disparity; incidence/age-adjusted rate) combined
with AND, using title/abstract/keyword fields and controlled vocabulary. Reviews,
letters, editorials, notes, and conference abstracts excluded. Reference lists of
included studies screened. Full strings in the protocol appendix (SEARCH_STRINGS_v2).

**13. URL to search strategy** — [attach SEARCH_STRINGS_v2.md / repository link]

**14. Condition or domain being studied** — Invasive female breast cancer
incidence (age-adjusted), United States.

**15. Participants / population**
Population-based (registry) female populations in the United States. Inclusion:
studies reporting age-adjusted invasive breast cancer incidence for ≥1 US racial/
ethnic minority group relative to non-Hispanic White women. Exclusion: mortality,
survival, prevalence, in-situ-only, stage-specific-only, male breast cancer,
mortality-to-incidence ratios, and non-US studies (retained only as narrative
international comparison).

**16. Intervention(s) / exposure(s)** — Race/ethnicity (self-identified or
registry-classified), including disaggregated ethnic subgroups, molecular
subtype, nativity, and age band.

**17. Comparator(s) / control** — Non-Hispanic White women (reference group).

**18. Types of study to be included**
Observational population-based / registry studies (cross-sectional incidence,
registry trend analyses) reporting age-adjusted incidence rates or rate ratios.
Excluded: reviews, editorials, letters, notes, conference abstracts, case reports.

**19. Context** — US cancer surveillance systems (SEER, NAACCR, USCS, state and
regional registries).

**20. Main outcome(s)**
Within-study incidence rate ratio (IRR) = minority age-adjusted incidence /
non-Hispanic White age-adjusted incidence. Timing/effect measures: age-adjusted
rates per 100,000; IRR with 95% CI.

**21. Additional outcome(s)**
Subtype-specific IRRs (HR+/HER2−, HR+/HER2+, HER2-enriched, triple-negative,
ER-status); age-band-specific rate ratios; nativity-stratified IRRs;
disaggregated Asian/Pacific Islander ethnic-subgroup IRRs.

**22. Data extraction (selection and coding)**
Two-stage screening (title/abstract, then full text) with an AI-assisted
pipeline; all decisions author-reviewed. Each extracted rate recorded with its
exact source location and verified by the author against the source-article PDF.
Data items: age-adjusted rates, IRR/RR, 95% CI or case counts/person-years,
registry, calendar period, race/ethnicity categories, subtype, age band.

**23. Risk of bias (quality) assessment**
Six domains tailored to registry incidence-by-race studies: ascertainment/
completeness, race/ethnicity classification, denominator accuracy, outcome
definition and standardization, comparability, and reporting/precision.

**24. Strategy for data synthesis**
Random-effects meta-analysis (DerSimonian–Laird) of within-study log-IRRs;
heterogeneity via I², τ², and 95% prediction intervals. Because US registries
overlap, aggregate pooled estimates are presented descriptively to characterize
heterogeneity, with inference placed on disaggregated strata. Sensitivity
analyses: fixed-effect, leave-one-out, drop-approximated-SE, and one-estimate-
per-registry-family. Certainty via a GRADE-style approach.

**25. Analysis of subgroups / subsets**
By detailed ethnicity (Asian/Pacific Islander subgroups), by molecular subtype,
by age band, and by nativity.

**26. Type and method of review** — Systematic review and meta-analysis
(quantitative synthesis of observational incidence data).
