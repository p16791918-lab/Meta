Introduction Breast cancer (BC) is a prevalent and highly fatal form of cancer that primarily affects women, ranking as the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [ 1 ]. China recorded 420,000 fresh instances of BC in 2020, representing 18.4% of new BC patients globally [ 2 ]. Based on the state of molecular profiles and microscopic characteristics, such as human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER2) amplification and estrogen receptor (ER) expression, BC can be classified as a heterogeneous disease [ 3 ]. The HER2 gene encodes for the HER2, a transmembrane tyrosine kinase receptor and an epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) family member. The family comprises EGFR/HER1, HER2, HER3, and HER4, facilitating signaling pathways associated with growth factors [ 4 ]. HER2 is generally expressed at low levels on the surfaces of epithelial cells, which is essential for the proper development of many organs such as the ovary, breast, liver, lung, and kidney. Immunohistochemical investigations have shown that HER2 is overexpressed in BC cells, with up to two million receptors in each cell. Elevated levels of HER2 in advanced BC are associated with decreased disease-free survival [ 5 ]. HER2-positive BC accounts for 15%-20% of BC and is characterized by its aggressive nature, high rates of recurrence, and tendency to spread to lymph nodes [ 6 ]. Trastuzumab is a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody (mAb) targeting domain four of the HER2 protein [ 7 ]. It is one of the most effective first-line targeted therapies for BC, which specifically targets and inhibits the growth of HER2-amplified BC cells without affecting tumors with normal levels of HER2 expression. Trastuzumab, when used in combination with other chemotherapy medications, enhances the prognosis of individuals with metastatic diseases and reduces the likelihood of cancer recurring and death [ 8 ]. However, many patients eventually develop resistance to trastuzumab. Following trastuzumab failure, subsequent therapeutic strategies include antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) and tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in combination with capecitabine [ 9 ]. ADCs, such as trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), consist of recombinant monoclonal antibody molecules synthetically bonded to a cytotoxic drug (drug payload or warheads) covalently. These agents combine the targeted delivery of monoclonal antibodies with the potent cytotoxic activity of chemotherapy, thereby providing substantial clinical benefit in HER2-positive BC [ 10 , 11 ]. In addition, TKIs such as lapatinib and pyrotinib, are small molecules that prevent access to the intracellular catalytic kinase domain of HER2. This prevents breast cancer cells from receiving the necessary signals for proliferation, thus impeding or eliminating the growth of tumor cells [ 12 ]. These agents, particularly when combined with capecitabine, have been shown to improve outcomes in patients with advanced HER2-positive BC [ 13 , 14 ]. Consistent with this therapeutic sequence, the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) Breast Cancer Guidelines recommend multiple advanced therapy regimens for patients with HER2-positive advanced BC who have experienced treatment failure with trastuzumab. These regimens include T-DM1, T-DXd, pyrotinib plus capecitabine, and lapatinib plus capecitabine [ 15 ]. Currently, there is a lack of extensive comparisons regarding the survival benefits of these approaches, and the cost-effectiveness of these regimens also remains unresolved. Hence, this study aimed to assess the survival outcomes and economics of these treatment regimens, offering valuable insights for clinical decision-making within Chinese medical institutions amidst the implementation of diagnosis-related groups (DRG). Additionally, it aims to provide evidence to support healthcare institutions and policy makers in optimizing resource allocation, while also informing clinicians of the relative economic value of available treatment regimens. Methods Model overview The partitioned survival model (PSM) was constructed using Microsoft Excel 2019 to assess the long-term cost impact of the T-DM1 group, T-DXd group, and pyrotinib plus capecitabine group compared to the lapatinib plus capecitabine group. The PSM incorporated three distinct states: progression-free (PF), progressive disease (PD), and death, based on the patient’s disease progression (Fig. 1 ) [ 16 ]. The progression-free survival (PFS) curve provides the proportion of patients in the PF state at time t , denoted as S(t)₂. The overall survival (OS) curve provides the proportion of patients alive at time t , denoted as S(t)₁. Accordingly, the proportion of patients in the death state is calculated as 1 − S(t)₁, whereas the proportion of patients in the PD state is derived from the difference between OS and PFS, expressed as PD(t) = S(t)₁ − S(t)₂ [ 17 ]. PSM was chosen because it is widely used in oncology economic evaluations and allows direct estimation of health state occupancy from OS and PFS data [ 18 ]. The model cycle length was 21 days, corresponding to a single cancer treatment cycle, and the time horizon was set to 10 years, reflecting the relatively low life expectancy of patients with advanced HER2-positive breast cancer [ 19 , 20 ]. The model outcomes included costs, life years (LYs), quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), and the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). The costs and clinical outcomes were discounted to the present by applying a 5% discount rate [ 21 ]. The World Health Organization recommends using three times per capita gross domestic product (GDP) as the threshold for willingness to pay (WTP) [ 22 ]– which, based on 2022 data [ 23 ], implies a Chinese WTP threshold of $35,220 (3 x $11,740). OS, overall survival; PFS, progression-free survival; PD, progressive disease Patient cohorts and treatment options The study assumed that the target population would be comparable to published clinical studies, such as NCT00829166 , NCT02422199 , NCT03080805 , and NCT03529110 [ 24 – 29 ]. The screening process could be found in Supplementary Table S1 . These trials were selected because they provide the most robust and comprehensive clinical efficacy data available for the regimens evaluated in HER2-positive advanced breast cancer. Detailed information for these studies is available at ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT00829166 ( https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00829166 ; conducted from February 2009 to July 2012), NCT02422199 ( https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT02422199 ; conducted from May 2015 to October 2016), NCT03080805 ( https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT03080805 ; conducted from May 2017 to March 2019), and NCT03529110 ( https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT03529110 ; conducted from August 2018 to May 2021) (Supplementary Table S2 ). The eligible participants were individuals who were 18 years of age or older, diagnosed with HER2-positive advanced BC through histological examination, and had received prior treatment with trastuzumab and a taxane. The therapy options for cohort patients are identical to those in clinical studies. In this study, the intervention group consisted of three subgroups: T-DXd, T-DM1, and pyrotinib in combination with capecitabine. T-DXd was administered intravenously every 3 weeks at a dosage of 5.4 mg/kg of body weight, T-DM1 was administered intravenously every 3 weeks at a dosage of 3.6 mg/kg, and pyrotinib was administered orally at a daily dose of 400 mg, along with oral capecitabine at 1000 mg/m² twice daily on days 1–14 of each 21-day cycle. Patients in the control group received oral lapatinib at a daily dosage of 1250 mg and oral capecitabine at 1000 mg/m² twice daily on days 1–14 of every 21-day cycle. For specific details, please refer to Supplementary Table S3 . In this study, lapatinib plus capecitabine was selected as the common comparator for two reasons. First, according to the CSCO Breast Cancer Guidelines, lapatinib plus capecitabine is one of the recommended treatment options after trastuzumab failure in patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer [ 15 ]. Second, among the included clinical trials, NCT00829166 , NCT02422199 , and NCT03080805 used lapatinib plus capecitabine as the comparator arm, which enabled the formation of a connected evidence network for anchored indirect treatment comparison. Therefore, lapatinib plus capecitabine was used as the common comparator in the present analysis. The average height and weight of Chinese women were 1.58 m and 59 kg, respectively [ 30 ]. Using the body surface area calculation formula, the average body surface area of Chinese women was determined to be 1.62 m². Therefore, the drug doses were adjusted based on this calculation for the cost analysis of the drug treatment [ 31 ]. Clinical inputs As raw survival data from the aforementioned clinical trials were unavailable, pseudo-individual patient data (pseudo-IPD) were reconstructed from published Kaplan–Meier survival curves using the algorithm proposed by Guyot et al. [ 32 ]. Coordinate points were first extracted using GetData Graph Digitizer (version 2.26), and the reconstructed datasets were then processed in R (version 4.3.0) to generate survival functions for each treatment arm (Supplementary Figure S1 , S2 ). The proportional hazards (PH) assumption was assessed using log–cumulative hazard plots. Because the PH assumption was not satisfied across all regimens, survival curves for each treatment were fitted independently using standard parametric distributions, Royston–Parmar (RP) spline models and fractional polynomial (FP) models under a frequentist framework (Supplementary Figure S3 , S4 ). In the absence of head-to-head comparisons among the four regimens, an anchored indirect treatment comparison (ITC) was performed using fractional polynomial (FP) models based on published survival data from pivotal randomized controlled trials (RCTs). Lapatinib plus capecitabine served as the overall anchor of the evidence network. Although it did not appear in all included trials, its presence in the pyrotinib trials ( NCT02422199 and NCT03080805 ) and in the EMILIA trial ( NCT00829166 ), together with the comparison of T-DM1 and T-DXd in NCT03529110 , enabled all regimens to be connected through a single anchored network via T-DM1. Each treatment arm’s survival curve was independently reconstructed and fitted using FP and RP models or eight common parametric approaches to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) relative to the anchor. The analysis was conducted under a frequentist framework, rather than a Bayesian one, because the available evidence comprised a small, well-connected network with limited prior information. The frequentist approach provided a transparent and computationally stable estimation for the model inputs. To extrapolate survival, FP models, RP models [ 16 , 33 , 34 ] and eight common parametric approaches (Weibull, exponential, gamma, log-normal, log-logistic, Gompertz, generalized gamma, and generalized F) were applied, thereby avoiding potentially unrealistic PH assumptions. The choice of the optimal fitting distribution was determined using the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). Given the tailing effect, clinical practice, and long-term epidemiological data, the distribution with the lowest BIC will be given priority in cases where the AIC and BIC values are inconsistent. Cost and utility inputs This study adopts a health system approach and focuses on direct medical expenditures, including drug acquisition costs, disease management costs, and adverse event (AE) management costs. Drug acquisition costs were based on the unit prices of the medications. Disease management costs covered expenditures related to routine follow-up, end-stage palliative care, and drug administration. The specific sources of drug acquisition costs and disease management costs are presented in Table 1 . Drug acquisition costs were primarily obtained from a local database (Yaozhi database), covering pyrotinib, capecitabine, lapatinib, T-DXd, and T-DM1, and all drug prices were based on 2023 data. Drug costs were converted from Chinese yuan to US dollars and calculated according to the dosage per 21-day cycle, with the results summarized in Table 1 . The remaining cost parameters, including disease management costs, were obtained from published literature. These literature-based cost inputs were used as reported in the original sources and were not further inflated to a single common price year, which may have introduced additional uncertainty into the economic estimates. AE management costs reflected the expenses required to treat specific toxicities. For each included RCT, the top three grade ≥ 3 AEs were identified based on incidence. After combining the data across all four trials, four distinct grade ≥ 3 AEs were included in the analysis: neutropenia/leukopenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, and nausea/diarrhoea. These events were selected because they represent the most costly events and are therefore the most likely to influence the results of the cost-effectiveness. The expenses related to AEs were calculated by the cost of managing AEs for each event and multiplying the frequency of each AE recorded in the clinical study. For regimens with AE data available from multiple trials, pooled incidence rates were estimated using weighted means. All AEs were assumed to occur at the onset of the simulation. Details of the included AEs are presented in Table 1 , and their incidence rates are summarised in Supplementary Table S4 . All costs were converted from Chinese yuan to US dollars using the exchange rate prevailing in November 2023 [ 35 ], where 1 USD was equivalent to 7.29 RMB. Table 1 presents the specifics of the cost parameters. As the clinical trials did not report any health-related quality of life (HRQoL) outcomes, utility values were obtained from previously published literature [ 36 – 38 ]. All patients treated under PF state received a utility value of 0.850; those entering PD status received a value of 0.685; and patients who died received a value of 0. Disutilities associated with AEs were not included in the model because robust AE-related disutility estimates were not available in the published literature. While these events are clinically significant, their overall frequency and duration are relatively limited compared with the long-term disease course, and thus the omission of AE-related disutilities is not expected to have a major impact on the overall cost-effectiveness conclusions. Table 1 presents the utility parameter details. Sensitivity analyses One-way sensitivity analyses were conducted by varying each model parameter individually while holding all others constant. Cost inputs and discount rates were varied by ± 20%, whereas HRs and utility values were varied by ± 10%, to assess the impact of parameter uncertainty on model stability. For the probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA), 1,000 Monte Carlo simulations were performed. In each simulation, random draws for all key model parameters were simultaneously sampled from their pre-specified probability distributions. Beta distributions were assigned to utilities because they are constrained between 0 and 1. Drug acquisition costs, disease management costs, and AE management costs were modeled using gamma distributions, as these cost components cannot take negative values. A normal distribution was applied to the remaining parameters. The ICER was calculated for each simulation to characterize overall parameter uncertainty. Ethical considerations The PFS/OS curve data were derived from previously cited trials rather than the database or medical records. Survival information was obtained from published Kaplan–Meier figures, and data reconstruction was conducted as detailed in the Methods section. Additionally, utilities and costs were sourced from published literature, obviating the need for ethics approval or specific consent procedures for this study. Indirect comparisons The simulation utilizing the FP model, namely the first-order FP with p = 0.5, was identified as the most accurate representation of the raw data. The HR for the lapatinib strategy was higher than that of the pyrotinib strategy. The HR for the T-DM1 strategy was lower than that of the pyrotinib strategy within the first year, and the HR for the T-DXd strategy was lower than that of the pyrotinib strategy within the first two years. At the 12-month mark, the HR was 1.37 for the lapatinib strategy, 0.98 for the T-DM1 strategy, and 0.61 for the T-DXd strategy. Due to impractical scenarios in the tail, this paper did not utilize any further data from the FP model fitting. The findings obtained from the FP model can be observed in Supplementary Table S7 . Base-case analysis In the base-case analysis, a 10-year time horizon was adopted to capture the full disease course for patients with advanced HER2-positive breast cancer. Model projections indicated that approximately 1% of patients remained alive at year 10 across all strategies, supporting this period as a practical lifetime horizon. All costs and outcomes were discounted at an annual rate of 5%. The detailed results of the base-case analysis are summarized in Table 2 . Lapatinib plus capecitabine had the lowest cost ($22,251.09) but also the lowest effectiveness (2.37 QALYs). T-DM1 and pyrotinib plus capecitabine yielded higher QALYs (3.27 and 3.42, respectively) at higher costs, while T-DXd provided the greatest benefit (4.63 QALYs) at the highest cost ($357,261.46). Sequential incremental analysis was performed by ordering strategies according to increasing cost, with lapatinib plus capecitabine as the baseline. The sequential ICERs were $50,029.87/QALY for T-DM1 versus lapatinib plus capecitabine, $114769.40/QALY for pyrotinib plus capecitabine versus T-DM1, and $225428.17/QALY for T-DXd versus pyrotinib plus capecitabine. At the WTP threshold of USD 35,220/QALY (three times China’s per-capita GDP in 2022), none of the three evaluated alternative regimens was cost-effective relative to lapatinib plus capecitabine. Importantly, although lapatinib generated fewer QALYs than pyrotinib, it was substantially less costly, reflecting the principle of decremental cost-effectiveness. Moreover, while T-DM1 and pyrotinib plus capecitabine were not cost-effective at this threshold, their ICERs relative to lapatinib plus capecitabine ($50,029.87/QALY and $59,278.37/QALY, respectively) were relatively close to the benchmark, suggesting that these regimens may still represent valuable alternatives under higher WTP threshold. In Fig. 3 , the horizontal axis represents incremental QALYs, and the vertical axis represents incremental costs, both calculated relative to lapatinib plus capecitabine as the common comparator. Points located in the northeast quadrant indicate that the regimen is associated with both higher costs and greater health benefits relative to lapatinib plus capecitabine. Each point represents one Monte Carlo simulation based on parameter uncertainty. The diagonal lines represent the WTP thresholds of $11,740/QALY, $35,220/QALY, and $70,440/QALY, corresponding to one, three, and six times the per capita GDP in China, respectively. Points below a given WTP line are considered cost-effective at that threshold, while points above the line are not. Figure 3 shows that most simulation points for pyrotinib plus capecitabine and T-DM1 were located above the $35,220/QALY WTP threshold but below the $70,440/QALY threshold, suggesting that these regimens were generally not cost-effective at the commonly used threshold of three times GDP per capita, although their probability of cost-effectiveness increased at higher WTP thresholds. In contrast, the simulation points for T-DXd were almost entirely above all three WTP lines, indicating a very low probability of cost-effectiveness under the current threshold settings. QALYs, quality-adjusted life years Discussion Trastuzumab has consistently been an effective therapy option for advanced HER2-positive breast cancer. However, as tumor targeting and chemotherapeutic technology continue to progress, numerous novel treatment alternatives have emerged to address the gap left by trastuzumab treatment failure. Due to their recent introduction, there is a shortage of ongoing studies evaluating the economic aspects of these drugs and treatment alternatives. The current study conducted a health economic analysis of the treatment pathway that follows the failure of trastuzumab in patients with advanced HER2-positive BC. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first comprehensive economic evaluation comparing multiple post-trastuzumab regimens, including pyrotinib plus capecitabine, lapatinib plus capecitabine, T-DM1, and T-DXd, for patients with advanced HER2-positive breast cancer from the perspective of a Chinese health system. The results of this study support the cost-effective distribution of scarce healthcare resources and offer valuable evidence for making therapeutic decisions. The base case analysis observed that compared to the lapatinib strategy, the alternative treatment options did not yield an ICER below the WTP threshold of $35,220/QALY. This indicates that, at the current WTP threshold, the alternative regimens are not considered cost-effective compared with lapatinib plus capecitabine, as they yielded higher ICERs and therefore do not provide good value for money at this threshold. The ICERs of the pyrotinib and T-DM1 strategies compared to lapatinib ($59,278.37/QALY and $50,029.87/QALY, respectively) were closer to the WTP threshold, suggesting that these regimens could become more cost-effective if their prices were reduced or if a higher WTP threshold were adopted. The ICER derived from comparing the T-DXd method with other strategies ($213,223.15 vs. T-DM1, $225,428.17 vs. pyrotinib plus capecitabine, and $148,234.68 vs. lapatinib plus capecitabine) significantly exceeded the WTP threshold, suggesting T-DXd medications are unlikely to exhibit cost-effectiveness, barring substantial cost reductions. One-way sensitivity analysis indicated that, relative to the lapatinib strategy, variations in utility values for the PF and PD health states, HRs for PFS and OS, patient body weight, and the drug acquisition costs of the experimental regimens were the key drivers of uncertainty in the economic outcomes. However, these factors did not alter the study results, thereby confirming the stability of the underlying case study findings. The PSA validated the robustness of the base case analysis findings and determined that T-DM1 may be preferred with an enhanced WTP threshold. Recent studies on the economic evaluation of second-line anti-HER2 treatments have mostly compared two options and thus lack comparisons of the cost-effectiveness of multiple second-line treatment options in the Chinese medical environment. Without sequential analysis, the benefits of including multiple comparators cannot be fully realized, which may limit the interpretation of their findings. Based on results from the DESTINY-Breast03 study, Youwen Zhu et al. conclude that T-DM1 is more cost-effective than T-DXd in the United States [ 15 ], while Jiangping Yan et al. [ 46 ], leveraging the same study, reach the opposite conclusion; importantly, neither analysis accounts for the unique features of the Chinese healthcare system. Mei Zhan et al. conducted a comparison between T-DXd and various chemotherapy regimens which were chosen by doctors. They determined that T-DXd, when used as a second-line treatment, was not cost-effective according to the Chinese medical system’s viewpoint [ 45 ]. The above research indicates that medical institutions have difficulty in identifying the most cost-effective approach, especially considering the actual situation in China. Our study addresses this gap by incorporating multiple second-line regimens following trastuzumab failure into a sequential ICER analysis, thereby providing a more comprehensive assessment of their comparative cost-effectiveness in the Chinese setting. In 2022, there were 357,000 newly diagnosed cases of breast cancer in China, ranking second among all cancers in women and accounting for 15.6% of all female cancer cases in the country and 15.5% of global breast cancer incidence in women [ 47 , 48 ]. These figures underscore the considerable disease burden of breast cancer in China, where incidence has been rising steadily. The age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) increased from 29.05 per 100,000 population in 2016 to 33.04 per 100,000 population in 2022 [ 47 , 49 ]. These trends highlight the growing clinical and economic burden of breast cancer in China. As HER2-positive breast cancer represents approximately 15–20% of all breast cancer cases [ 6 ], assessing the cost-effectiveness of anti-HER2 regimens is crucial for optimizing resource allocation and informing clinical practice in the Chinese healthcare system. Based on the base-case analysis, the T-DXd regimen was shown to have the best therapeutic effect, yielding 6.02 life-years and 4.63 QALYs, which represents a gain of nearly three life-years and more than two QALYs compared with lapatinib plus capecitabine (3.34 LYs and 2.37 QALYs). The pyrotinib plus capecitabine regimen also demonstrated longer survival (4.82 LYs and 3.42 QALYs) than lapatinib, while T-DM1 achieved intermediate benefits (4.35 LYs and 3.27 QALYs). However, in the NCT03080805 trial, only PFS Kaplan–Meier curves were available for pyrotinib plus capecitabine versus lapatinib plus capecitabine, as the OS data were immature. Therefore, OS inputs in our model were derived from the NCT02422199 trial, while PFS data were taken from the NCT03080805 trial and extrapolated accordingly. In NCT02422199 , the OS difference between treatment arms was not statistically significant ( p = 0.18), and our reconstructed curves yielded similar results ( p = 0.24), likely due to the small sample size and limited follow-up of this Phase II study. In contrast, the Phase III NCT03080805 trial showed a significant PFS benefit ( p 0.0001), and our fitted curves were consistent with this finding. Thus, the increased life-years associated with pyrotinib plus capecitabine in our model are primarily driven by the PFS improvement. The superior outcomes of T-DXd observed in our model are consistent with the outcomes of clinical trials [ 28 , 29 ] and several other cost-effectiveness analyses [ 31 , 45 ]. The survival advantage of T-DXd may be attributed to its higher antibody-to-drug ratio (8 vs. 3–4) than T-DM1, and its payload allows deruxtecan to enter the cell membrane of HER 2 + tumor cells or adjacent cells without the requirement for significant levels of HER 2 expression [ 50 ]. While T-DXd demonstrated the most substantial clinical benefit, it was less cost-effective than alternative regimens in this investigation. Importantly, at the time of this analysis, T-DXd had not yet been included in the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL), which limited its affordability in China. With its addition to the 2025 NRDL and the anticipated price reduction, however, T-DXd may achieve improved cost-effectiveness in future evaluations. Beyond cost-effectiveness, the affordability of introducing novel HER2 targeted therapies is also influenced by their budget impact. Previous research has shown that budget impact analyses (BIAs) provide complementary insights to cost-effectiveness evaluations by assessing the economic consequences for healthcare systems of adopting anticancer drugs [ 51 – 54 ]. A recent systematic review further reported considerable cross-country variation in the budgetary effects of breast cancer medications and emphasized that methodological frameworks, collaboration with pharmaceutical companies, and the chosen time horizons can markedly influence BIA outcomes [ 55 ]. Therefore, future evaluations in China should conduct high-quality, real-world, long-term BIAs alongside cost-effectiveness analyses to better inform both the economic value and financial sustainability of emerging HER2 targeted regimens and to support evidence-based budget allocation. Although this study was conducted from the perspective of the Chinese health system, it has broader international relevance. First, it addresses an important issue in oncology: the cost-effectiveness of HER2-targeted treatment strategies after trastuzumab failure in HER2-positive advanced breast cancer. This issue is relevant across healthcare settings, particularly where resource constraints and reimbursement policies vary. Second, the methodological framework of this study, including the PSM and associated sensitivity analyses, aligns with international standards for cost-effectiveness analysis and may be applied in other settings using local cost data and decision thresholds. Third, this analysis illustrates how cost-effectiveness analysis can support decision-making even in the absence of head-to-head trial data. However, the cost-effectiveness estimates reported here are context-dependent and are strongly influenced by local drug prices, healthcare resource use, and WTP thresholds. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted primarily as evidence of the trade-off between efficacy and affordability, while the economic conclusions should be re-evaluated using local data and decision thresholds in specific healthcare settings. Several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting the findings of this study. First, no single historical standard comparator was available to directly connect all four regimens within the current evidence base. Therefore, the present analysis used lapatinib plus capecitabine as the common comparator within a connected evidence network and relied on anchored indirect comparisons across multiple randomized controlled trials. Accordingly, the findings should be interpreted as relative cost-effectiveness comparisons within the available evidence network rather than absolute cost-effectiveness estimates for all four regimens against a unified historical standard comparator. In addition, heterogeneity among the included studies may have introduced bias into the estimated treatment effects. Second, some uncertainty may exist regarding the extrapolation of OS beyond the trial follow-up period, as long-term outcomes were modeled using parametric assumptions rather than real-world data. Third, the utility values applied for the PF and PD states were derived from published literature rather than patient-level data, which may not fully capture quality-of-life differences among regimens. Fourth, for the pyrotinib plus capecitabine regimen, the PFS data were obtained from the NCT03080805 trial whereas the OS data were sourced from the NCT02422199 study, potentially introducing bias due to differences in trial design and outcome maturity. Moreover, adverse event–related disutilities were not explicitly modeled owing to the lack of robust estimates in the literature; however, this simplification is unlikely to materially affect the overall cost-effectiveness results given their limited frequency and duration. Finally, the model assumed that patients did not switch or discontinue treatment during the simulation, which may differ from real-world practice and should be considered when interpreting the results. Conclusion Within the analytical framework and available connected evidence network, none of the three evaluated alternative regimens demonstrated cost-effectiveness relative to lapatinib plus capecitabine at the current WTP threshold for Chinese patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer after trastuzumab treatment failure. Although pyrotinib plus capecitabine and T-DM1 provided improved clinical outcomes, their higher costs limited their economic attractiveness compared with lapatinib plus capecitabine. T-DXd demonstrated the greatest clinical benefit but was associated with the least favorable economic profile at the time of this analysis. Highlights Following trastuzumab treatment failure, patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer usually continue treatment with antibody-drug conjugates and tyrosine kinase inhibitors, including lapatinib plus capecitabine, pyrotinib plus capecitabine, trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1), and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd). However, recent cost-effectiveness studies have mostly compared only two regimens at a time and were rarely conducted in the Chinese healthcare context, leaving a lack of comprehensive comparative evidence relevant to China. In the current Chinese healthcare context and within the available connected evidence network, none of the three evaluated alternative regimens was cost-effective relative to lapatinib plus capecitabine at prevailing willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds. Although T-DM1, pyrotinib plus capecitabine, and T-DXd provided improved clinical outcomes relative to lapatinib plus capecitabine, their substantially higher costs limited their economic attractiveness. Within China’s current healthcare setting, lapatinib combined with capecitabine represents a more affordable therapeutic option for many patients. Nevertheless, T-DXd demonstrates the most substantial clinical benefit, and in situations where cost is less of a constraint, it may be considered to achieve improved clinical outcomes.
